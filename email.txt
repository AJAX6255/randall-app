3. Hand‑crafted classification rules (simple, transparent)
Before you jump to machine learning, you can build a straightforward rules‑based tagger:

First prerequisite: big enough oil move

Only classify days when |Δ oil| > threshold (e.g., > 2 standard deviations of its 60‑day vol) so you focus on meaningful shocks.

Supply‑shock (oil‑shock inflation regime) conditions (example):

Oil ↑ strongly

SPY ≤ 0 (flat to down) over the same day or short window

VIX ↑ noticeably (e.g., > +10%)

DSG10 ↑ or mixed (inflation worry)

Tag: regime = "oil_supply"

Demand‑shock (growth‑demand regime) conditions (example):

Oil ↑ strongly

SPY ↑ (e.g., > 0.3–0.5%)

VIX flat or ↓

DSG10 ↑ modestly (growth) or breakevens ↑ more than real yields

Tag: regime = "oil_demand"

Ambiguous / no‑shock regime:

If conditions above are not met, tag regime = "mixed" or None.

This essentially encodes the sign‑restriction intuition that central banks and academics use, but in a live, heuristic style.

4. Turning it into a small statistical model
Once you’re comfortable with the logic, you can step up to a simple statistical classifier:

Create labels (training target)

Use your rules above to generate preliminary labels on historical data (say 10–15 years).

Manually spot‑check known episodes (e.g., 2008 demand collapse, 2014–2016 oil collapse, 2020 COVID, recent OPEC cuts) and adjust thresholds so the labels look sensible.

Define features
For each date t (or week t):

x1: standardized Δ oil (returns).

x2: standardized Δ SPY.

x3: standardized Δ VIX.

x4: Δ DSG10 (bps).

Optional extras: Δ copper, Δ breakevens, Δ HY spread.

Train a simple model

Start with logistic regression (multi‑class: supply vs demand vs other).

Or a low‑depth decision tree / random forest so you can inspect decision rules.

The model then outputs P(supply), P(demand), P(other) each day.

Interpretation and overlay with your dashboard

Map regime → your heuristic side:

P(supply) high → use “oil‑shock inflation regime” heuristics (oil ↑, USD ↑, GLD pressured, SPY ↓, VIX ↑).

P(demand) high → use “growth‑demand regime” heuristics (oil ↑ with SPY ↑, VIX subdued; GLD behavior depends more on real yields).

This is very similar in spirit to daily/weekly VAR‑based decompositions and factor models used in the literature, but without their heavy identification machinery.
